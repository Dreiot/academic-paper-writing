# Reviewer revision

Use this phase after submission to analyze editor and reviewer comments, revise the manuscript, and prepare a response letter when requested. The manuscript must remain a standalone paper, not a visible transcript of the review exchange.

## Use the actual revision record

Read the editor decision, all current reviewer comments, the current manuscript, and any previous response letter needed to understand the round. If a required artifact is unavailable, request that specific artifact; do not infer the missing comment or manuscript state.

For each comment, privately identify:

- the explicit request;
- the scientific concern actually supported by the wording of the comment;
- the relevant manuscript passage and available evidence;
- the appropriate response type;
- the exact manuscript change, if any.

This private analysis is not a mandatory user-facing matrix. Surface it only when the user asks for an analysis or a decision is needed.

## Choose a response proportionate to the comment

Use one of four routes:

1. **Correct or revise:** the comment identifies a factual, methodological, evidential, logical, or material presentation problem.
2. **Clarify:** the manuscript already contains the substance, but its logic, definition, scope, or location is unclear.
3. **Add authorized evidence:** a new analysis or experiment is genuinely needed and the user has authorized it; do not claim completion before it exists.
4. **Respectfully disagree:** the requested change is inconsistent with the method, evidence, scope, or journal requirements. Explain the reason directly and add a narrow clarification when useful.

If the reviewer request, manuscript claim, and evidence conflict or the intended claim is unclear, use the Claim Decision process. Do not automatically accept, reject, or weaken the claim.

## Answer the comment, not imagined future reviewers

- Treat the supplied reviewer comment as a real objection. Address the explicit request and only the underlying scientific issue necessary to resolve it.
- Do not speculate about other concerns the reviewer might raise in the next round.
- Do not add unrelated limitations, experiments, citations, ethics discussion, or robustness claims to look comprehensive.
- Do not convert every reviewer preference into a scientific defect.
- When comments overlap, keep each response identifiable while avoiding duplicated manuscript text.

## Integrate changes naturally into the manuscript

- Write the revised passage for a future reader, not as a reply to the reviewer.
- Avoid phrases such as `in response to the reviewer` in the manuscript unless the journal explicitly requires a revision note.
- Place definitions, motivation, limitations, and results where they belong scientifically instead of appending defensive paragraphs.
- Preserve the existing structure when a local repair is sufficient. Restructure only when the comment exposes a real argument-level problem.

## Write a concise, credible response letter

For each comment, normally:

1. answer the question or state the position directly;
2. explain the evidence or reasoning needed for that answer;
3. state the concrete manuscript change;
4. give the verified section, page, line, table, or figure location when available.

Use brief, natural thanks when appropriate; do not begin every response with exaggerated agreement or praise. Do not repeat the full comment unless the journal requires it. When disagreeing, remain factual and specific rather than defensive or adversarial.

Do not report a change, experiment, line number, or result that has not been verified in the final revision. If no manuscript change is warranted, explain why in the shortest scientifically complete form.

## Validate the revision set

- Every explicit editor and reviewer request has a traceable response.
- Every claimed manuscript change exists in the delivered version and at the cited location.
- Manuscript, response letter, supplement, tables, figures, and numerical results agree.
- Revisions do not introduce a stronger unsupported claim or contradict another section.
- New explanations read naturally and do not duplicate defensive language elsewhere.
- The response does not answer questions the reviewers did not ask.
- Clean, marked, and response-letter versions are produced only when requested or required by the journal.
