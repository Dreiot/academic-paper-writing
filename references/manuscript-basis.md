# Manuscript basis and field conventions

Use this reference for a complete manuscript or substantive revision. Establish the writing context before selecting an outline or exemplars, and retain the reusable basis locally so later revisions do not reconstruct it from chat summaries. This is writing support within the authorized task, not a research audit or an additional approval stage.

## Identify the writing context

Infer from the manuscript, author instructions, study records, and suitable primary literature:

- the research field and specific question, including distinct contributing fields when relevant;
- the study and article type, intended readers, target venue when selected, and applicable reporting framework;
- the main contribution and the roles of the available evidence, such as observations, interventions, theory, qualitative accounts, simulations, or method comparisons;
- the conventions that matter here: terminology, argument order, evidence presentation, figure and table use, and the responsibilities of discussion and conclusion.

These are decision criteria, not required headings or a questionnaire. Use an explicit, reversible working classification when the material is sufficient; ask only when a consequential ambiguity cannot be resolved. An unfamiliar term, a prestigious journal, or one shared statistical technique does not determine the field or article type. Follow [literature-and-rhetoric.md](literature-and-rhetoric.md#match-sources-to-their-role) when choosing evidence and exemplars.

Keep the research question and the strength of its answer distinct. An inconclusive result can limit an explanation without erasing the object, pathway, hypothesis, or design objective actually studied. Preserve that focus while calibrating the conclusion to direct evidence under the existing [Claim Decision boundary](../SKILL.md#escalate-evidence-claim-conflicts-to-the-user).

## Keep one reusable local file

For the work covered by this reference, reuse a suitable existing manuscript note or evidence record; otherwise create one file in the manuscript or project's established writing/documentation folder, for example `manuscript-basis.md` or `写作依据与文献学习.md`. Use one basis per manuscript when a project contains multiple papers. Respect an explicit no-write request or supplied closed corpus; discussion, plans, pure formatting, and local copyediting alone do not authorize creating this record or expanding the search.

Keep only information useful for current writing and later continuation. The following contents can share sections or tables; omit inapplicable fields rather than filling a fixed template:

| Content | Retain |
| --- | --- |
| Writing context | Field, study/article type, readers, research question, contribution, and relevant venue conventions; distinguish author decisions, observed conventions, and official requirements. |
| Current inputs | Author-designated draft and supplement, relevant result and figure sources, and stable versions or locations. Point to originals rather than duplicating raw data or full reports. |
| Scientific support | Source identity and DOI or stable link, actual reading scope, exact supporting location, supported proposition, consequential conditions, and relevant conflicting evidence. Distinguish literature facts, local findings, and interpretation. |
| Exemplar learning | Source location, argumentative or visual function, transferable practice, why it fits this manuscript, where it is used, and any material mismatch. Paraphrase the practice rather than copying distinctive wording or full articles. |
| Evidence presentation | Questions answered by existing observations, analyses, images, figures, or tables; their main-text/supplement roles and source pointers. Include only assets needed for the argument. |
| Effective decisions | The short, actionable [writing brief](literature-and-rhetoric.md#learn-how-strong-papers-construct-the-argument), settled terminology, narrative choices and their reasons, protected facts and scope, and any author-approved style sample or preference. Keep paper-specific requirements local. |
| Active obligations | Unresolved author/reviewer requests or material gaps, relevant passages/sources, and the final artifact location once implementation is verified. A change note alone cannot close an item. |

Connect the judgments that carry the paper's argument through **research question -> supported judgment -> direct evidence -> necessary explanation and conditions -> manuscript or figure/table location**. Keep material counterevidence beside the judgment it affects. Use the existing citations, result identifiers, and source pointers; this is a way to organize the relevant entries, not a separate claim registry or a map of every sentence. Update only the affected links as writing proceeds.

For consequential rhetorical learning, retain the short chain **source location -> function -> applicability -> manuscript use**, then synthesize applicable practices into the shared writing brief. Merely naming a paper or describing its prose as clear does not preserve what was learned. This record is not a substitute for actually studying the relevant evidence and writing.

## Maintain the basis without duplicating authority

Create or update the relevant entries during authorized writing; do not add a separate documentation phase. On continuation, read the current inputs and still-effective decisions, reuse valid source checks, and reopen only affected items when evidence, author intent, or the venue changes. Replace superseded working summaries with the effective position and a necessary reason or pointer; retain important negative evidence and consequential decisions without accumulating a turn-by-turn diary.

The file is a finding aid and writing record. Direct sources control facts, the latest author-designated manuscript controls the textual baseline, active author instructions control the requested revision, and existing project authorities control approved research scope and claims. Resolve a conflict from those sources rather than letting the note silently redefine them. Maintaining the file does not authorize experiments, new statistical analyses, claim promotion, Git operations, or new project authority files.

Keep the basis outside the reader-facing paper and supplement. Retain it locally; committing, uploading, or sharing it follows the user's authorization and project rules. Deliver the requested writing, identifying the basis location when useful rather than automatically appending its contents. Use the same file for [literature traceability](literature-and-rhetoric.md#keep-private-working-evidence), [author revision obligations](author-revision.md#keep-a-proportionate-revision-record), and [candidate integration](manuscript-quality-control.md#keep-one-authoritative-candidate); create separate records only when they serve a distinct necessary purpose.
